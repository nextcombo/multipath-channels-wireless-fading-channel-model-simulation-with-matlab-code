The Rayleigh channel model is one of the most commonly used small-scale fading models, used to describe the fading channels in urban environments. Due to the numerous buildings in urban areas, signals cannot directly reach the receiver from the transmitter but must pass through multiple paths such as reflections, refraction, and diffraction before reaching the receiver. It is assumed that all arriving waves have arbitrary directions and that the average power across all paths is the same.
In the Rayleigh fading model, the amplitudes of the multipath components arriving at the same time are random, hence the combined received signal amplitude is also random at any given moment. It should be noted that throughout the fading process, only a very small portion of deep fades with larger amplitudes occur, and in most cases, only moderate to minor fading occurs. These minor amplitude fadings correspond to higher probability signals, thus their time-varying and stochastic amplitudes have statistical regularities. The angles of arrival of the multipath components are equally likely in the horizontal direction, so their phases follow an uniform distribution.

Probability density functions are typically used to describe the likelihood of receiving a particular amplitude signal at any given moment. The time domain plot and first-order statistical characteristics of amplitude and phase probability density distributions are shown in Figure 4. In the time domain plot, it can be seen that the signal strength is not fixed but exhibits significant fading phenomena, mostly consisting of small to moderate amplitude fadings below 10dB, along with some large amplitude deep fades. Regarding first-order statistical characteristics, the amplitude probability density function follows a Rayleigh distribution, while the phase probability density function follows an uniform distribution. Other channel parameters related to the Rayleigh model include delay, signal strength, moving speed, carrier frequency, phase shift, etc.
